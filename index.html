LIVE SYSTEM — RESPONDS TO CURSOR

v1 Reflection

The strongest rule from v1 was the curve-drawing behavior — slow, deliberate cursor movement producing soft curling lines and circular nodes felt immediately legible and matched the tone I wanted. The weakest part was the geometric shatter effect at high speed: I noted in my v1 End of Build Reflection that it had no real limit, and could clutter the canvas with fragments the longer someone moved quickly. For v2, I'm removing the shatter behavior entirely and tightening the one remaining rule instead — specifically the threshold between "slow" and "fast" movement, so the shift between building a curve and losing it feels like a clean, controlled state change rather than two competing effects. My question going into this build is how much character a single rule can carry once it no longer has a second, contrasting behavior to lean on.

End of Build Reflection

What can it do now that the tests couldn't? In the tests, each behavior lived in its own isolated canvas, reacting to its own separate input. Now the sigil holds two behaviors at once, driven by a single shared input — cursor speed — so the system reads as one coherent thing responding to one gesture, instead of four unrelated demos sitting next to each other.

What feels most "you" about the behavior? The way it punishes fast, careless movement and only rewards patience feels the most deliberate. It doesn't respond evenly to any input — it has a clear preference for stillness and calm, and reacts sharply the moment that's broken.

Where does it drift into generic or noisy? The jagged, spiky shape at high speed is the part that risks feeling generic — "shape gets messy when you move the mouse fast" is a fairly common effect, and right now it doesn't yet feel specific to this system versus any other reactive sketch.

What will you change in v2? I want to replace the generic jitter/spike effect with something more specific to this system's identity — for example, the sigil breaking into a fixed number of pieces that scatter and have to reassemble during stillness, rather than just becoming visually noisy.
